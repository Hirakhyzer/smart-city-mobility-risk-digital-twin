# Governance and Ethics

The **Smart City Mobility Risk Digital Twin** is an independent synthetic planning simulator for studying urban mobility risk, congestion, accident-risk signals, emissions burden, emergency-route delays, and transport-equity trade-offs.

## 1. Intended use

Acceptable uses include:

- Studying smart-city mobility planning with fictional synthetic data.
- Comparing transparent policy scenarios before using real city data.
- Teaching digital twins, urban computing, transport analytics, and responsible AI planning.
- Prototyping congestion, safety, emissions, emergency-delay, and equity metrics.
- Building reproducible reports and audit trails for planning research.

## 2. Non-intended use

The project is not intended for:

- Live traffic-signal control.
- Emergency dispatch or emergency-route decision automation.
- Road closures, public warnings, policing, surveillance, or enforcement.
- Official city policy certification.
- Infrastructure investment decisions without human governance.
- Road-safety, environmental, transport, or legal compliance certification.

## 3. Planning boundary

The simulator uses fictional zones, roads, transit stops, emergency facilities, vehicles, incidents, weather patterns, and policy scenarios. Synthetic outputs are research signals only and should not be presented as measured city performance.

## 4. Equity principle

Mobility analytics can affect communities differently. Equity metrics should be interpreted with local context, accessibility needs, public participation, demographic safeguards, and review by transport and community stakeholders.

## 5. Emergency-services principle

Emergency-delay outputs are review prompts, not dispatch guidance. Any real emergency-routing work requires emergency-services validation, fail-safe design, operational testing, and clear accountability.

## 6. Privacy principle

Real mobility traces can expose sensitive location patterns, home/work inference, health visits, school travel, religious attendance, and protected activities. Real data integration requires minimization, aggregation, retention limits, privacy review, and security controls.

## 7. Responsible deployment boundary

Moving from this prototype to real planning requires calibrated traffic data, field validation, transport-engineering review, environmental review, public consultation, cybersecurity review, accessibility review, legal review, and formal city governance.

## 8. Responsible statement

This lab supports learning, simulation, and research. It should never be used as the sole basis for real traffic control, emergency routing, road closures, public warnings, policing, infrastructure spending, or official policy decisions.
